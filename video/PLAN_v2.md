# Urbora Round 1 video, v2: production plan

The rough cut v0 was calm and slow: long locked-off shots, no sound, no reason to keep watching after
second five. v2 is rebuilt around attention. The story and the honesty rules stay the same.

## 1. How attention works for this audience (the brief behind every decision)

The judges watch many two-minute videos in a row, on a laptop or phone, often with the sound low.
Their attention is spent the way everyone's is now: they judge a video in its first three seconds,
and after that they decide again every few seconds whether to keep watching.

| Principle | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **Hook in 1.5 s** | Open on something specific and slightly unsettling: a dying rice shoot, snap zoom, a real number ("৫০ মণ → ৩০ মণ"). No logos or titles first. |
| **Open a loop, close it late** | "কেন হয় না?" is asked at 0:15. The answer (মাজরা পোকা, 20 gram for 2 bigha) lands at 1:05. People stay to hear the answer. |
| **Something changes every 1.5–3 s** | A cut, a text beat, a sound, a camera move. Average shot length is about 2.4 s in the fast acts. Long holds are kept for the three emotional beats, and they work *because* the rest is fast. |
| **Contrast drives feeling** | Fast → sudden silence → reveal. Green dawn → gold harvest. Many answers → one answer. |
| **Sound first** | Cuts land on the music's beats. Whooshes, clicks, the phone buzz and one-bar silences mark the turns. Narration is a human voice (Jawat's), never a robotic narrator. |
| **Built for muted viewing** | Key Bangla words appear large as kinetic type, with English subtitles at the bottom. A viewer with the sound off still follows the story. |
| **Specific beats generic** | Real figures from the real system and a real farmer's words, not adjectives. |
| **Visual rhymes** | Each of these returns later: the dawn walk and the gold walk, the shop counter before and after, the bank road and the field road rolling up. The viewer's brain rewards the repeat. |
| **Peak-end rule** | The strongest feeling comes at the end (the daughter in gold paddy, the flute theme returning, the name), because that is what the judges remember when they score. |
| **Show the product early** | The name appears at 0:41 and the real app within 50 s. The judges score function. |

## 2. Structure (beat grid 100 BPM: 1 beat = 0.6 s, 1 bar = 2.4 s)

| Time | Act | Pace | What happens |

| 0:07–0:19 | **The land** | slow, beautiful | An FPV drone skims green boro at sunrise, then the dawn walk. VO: "এ দেশের মাটি উর্বর…" then "কিন্তু একই জমিতে…" on the dry-shoot macro. |

## 3. Voice-over (Jawat's cloned voice, ElevenLabs engine; spoken Bangla, not bookish)

Delivery: a 19-year-old telling a friend something he cares about. Warm, a little urgent in the
hook, relaxed in the land section, confident in the proof. Never announcer-like.

1. দিনাজপুরের এক কৃষক আমাদের বলছিলেন, গতবার পঞ্চাশ মণ ধান পাইছিলেন। এইবার তিরিশ।
2. এ দেশের মাটি উর্বর। এই মাটিই আমাদের সবচেয়ে বড় উপহার।
3. কিন্তু একই জমিতে এখন আর আগের মতো ধান হয় না।
4. কেন হয় না? প্রশ্ন একটাই। উত্তর অনেক।
5. (character voices, quick) দোকানি: "এইটা নিয়া যান, এইবার সবাই এইটাই নিতেছে।" · প্রতিবেশী: "আমি তো পটাশ দিছিলাম, কাজ হইছে।" · ভিডিও: "এইটা দিলেই হয়!" · স্মৃতি: "আব্বা সবসময় এইটাই দিত।"
6. কার কথা শুনবো?
7. উপজেলা কৃষি অফিসে যাইতেই পুরা দিন লাগে।
8. টাকা পাঠাইতে এখন আর ব্যাংকে যাইতে হয় না। তাইলে কৃষির পরামর্শের জন্য কেন?
9. উর্বরা। কৃষি বিশেষজ্ঞ, এখন আপনার ফোনে।
10. একটা কল দেন। উর্বরা নিজেই জিজ্ঞেস করে নেয়: কত দিনের চারা, কোন অংশে সমস্যা।
11. (real call audio from the app)
12. উত্তর আসে SMS-এ: কী রোগ, কোন ওষুধ, আর আপনার জমির মাপে ঠিক কতটুকু।
13. (দোকানি) ও, মাজরা পোকা? তাইলে এইটা নেন, এইটুকুই লাগবে।
14. শুধু বৃষ্টির খবর না। কী করবেন, সেইটাও বলে দেয়।
15. উর্বরা বানায়া কিছু বলে না। হিসাব আসে পরীক্ষিত কোড থেকে। না জানলে সোজা বলে দেয়।
16. স্মার্টফোন হোক বা বাটন ফোন। দিন হোক বা রাত।
17. মাটির যত্ন নিলে, স্বপ্ন ফলে। (end card) উর্বরা। স্বপ্ন ফলুক।

## 5. Sound

- Music: a folk-fusion score at 100 BPM (bansuri, dotara, dhol, strings) with the structure above,
  one track so the cuts can follow its beats. Open: see the question to the team.
- Sound design: a snap on each hook cut, whooshes on the roll-ups, a phone buzz, a keypad beep, a
  paper-stamp thud when the seal lands, rain and wind, a soft tick per node in the trust diagram.
- Mix: VO around −16 LUFS integrated, music ducked 8–10 dB under the voice, final around −14 LUFS.

## 6. The loop (every step is generated, checked, then fixed)

1. Generate → 2. Check against a written checklist (below) with extracted frames, speech-to-text
on every voice line, beat detection on the music → 3. Fix only what fails → 4. Show the team.

Checklist for every shot: no text or logos in generated footage; no faces; paddy is rice, not
wheat; the ring and gamcha stay consistent; no morphing in the frames used; one clear subject; the
move reads at phone size. For the whole cut: a hook in the first 1.5 s; no shot longer than 6 s
except the three emotional holds; subtitles readable at 0.3 s per word; ends at or before 2:00; no
"draft" text or watermarks; honesty labels in the credits line.

## 7. What the three Grameenphone references teach (analysed 2026-09-30)

Measured with speech-to-text timings, pitch tracking and beat/key detection. The voices are real people's; we match their *style* with licensed voices and do not clone them.

- **Story first, narrator last.** "মান-অভিমান" (2:18) is told entirely by a character in dialect; the brand narrator speaks only at 1:58–2:13, four short lines ("বন্ধু মানে মুখে বলা নয় / বন্ধু মানে পাশে থাকা…"). "কথাগুলো হারিয়ে না যাক" (0:50) is a real-life moment, then 6 s of narrator.
- **Narrator profile:** deep baritone (median ≈98 Hz), lively pitch (≈3.9 semitone spread), 4–6-word phrases at ≈2.5–3 words/s, a clear pause after each phrase, close and warm, never shouted.
- **Music:** warm major keys (A, D, B major); light under the story; the anthem ("এক আমি", 99 BPM) builds steadily and peaks at the end.

**What changes in our film:** Mahmud tells his own story in first person, in spoken village Bangla (a warm, livelier male voice). The narrator (a deep, calm voice) speaks only at the turn, a few proof lines and the close. The music stays light and rhythmic under the problem and builds into an anthem-like swell from the reveal to the end card.

## 9. Voice loop

Targets: narrator median F0 95–115 Hz, spread ≥3.3 st, 2.6–3.0 words/s inside a phrase; Mahmud 115–135 Hz, spread ≥3.5 st, 2.8–3.2 words/s (livelier, a younger farmer); every line ≥0.97 speech-to-text similarity. Loop: synthesise → measure → adjust pace/voice → re-synthesise, at most three rounds per voice, then pick the closest.
