সুদিন means "good days". The name carries the promise: the yields a family remembers can come back, when someone finally tells them what is actually wrong with the crop.

The essence

Farmers are already trying. Expert help is just too far away.

We are not selling information the farmer lacks. He already asks the shop, his neighbours and the agriculture office. What he can't get easily is an expert who can name the disease or nutrient gap and tell him what to do about it. Sudin brings that expert to the phone in his hand.

Colour · research-led

A sunrise over a healthy field

The old palette came from four separate ideas: government green, telecom blue, a gold and an ivory. That is why it felt detached. The new one is a single scene with one light source. Deep crop green holds it steady, turmeric sunlight is the warmth that draws people in, and everything else comes from the same field.

গভীর শ্যামল#0D3B2E
Primary · ~30%Deep Shyamol

Identity, headers, primary buttons on light. A darker, bluer green than the flag's #006A4E, so it reads as a considered private brand rather than a ministry portal. Dark, low-saturation greens read as calm, stable and safe.

How colour builds trust here

  • Hue, saturation and brightness shift what people believe about a brand. In controlled experiments, blue raised perceived competence and red and orange raised excitement (Labrecque & Milne, 2012). Colour causes these impressions; it does not just decorate them.
  • We need two feelings at once. Safe (can I trust this with my crop?) comes from the deep, low-saturation green. Drawn in (I want to open this) comes from the warm, saturated turmeric. Calm, then warmth.
  • Familiar colours read as "ours". Green is the fertile countryside. Yellow and marigold are Falgun, mustard fields and wedding haldi: renewal and good times. Our name is good times.
  • Being different helps people remember you. Wise left "safe blue" for green and saw conversion rise about 10%. Our sector is flat government green or tech blue, so the turmeric sun makes us easy to spot.
  • Restraint reads as confidence. Two identity colours, one signature. Every extra colour costs trust.

Each concept is drawn on a 64-unit grid as clean vector. It is tested on light and dark, as an app icon, at favicon size and in one colour. Each one pairs with a different Bangla wordmark face, so you can judge the type too.

Words · the brand line

Two words, for years

Like GP's "চলো বহুদূর": short, spoken, pointing forward, and bigger than any one feature. The line is not about getting back what was lost. It is about the future a farming family is working towards: a good harvest, a child's schooling, a pucca house. Each option below has been said aloud next to the name.

L1স্বপ্ন ফলুক"May your dreams bear fruit." ফলা means both "to grow a harvest" and "to come true", so one word holds the field and the future. It is a wish, warm like a blessing from an elder, and it works for decades: first rice, then fish, poultry, the next generation. Said with the name: সুদিন · স্বপ্ন ফলুক.Recommended
L2সোনা ফলাও"Grow gold." It echoes সোনার ধান and "এ মাটিতে সোনা ফলে". Proud, an imperative like "চলো". It leans on harvest more than on the wider future.
L3আসছে সুদিন"Good days are coming." Hopeful and very easy to remember, but it repeats the name, so it can't sit beside the logo. Better as a campaign or video end line.
L4জেনে ফলাও"Know, then grow." The clearest link to what the product does (knowing the problem first). Less emotional, more practical.
L5নতুন ভোর"A new dawn." It matches logo A's rising sun. Poetic, but it doesn't say farming by itself.
How the line is used. Under the logo, at the end of every video, and as the sign-off of SMS replies ("— সুদিন · স্বপ্ন ফলুক"). Problem lines such as "ফসলের কী হয়েছে, জানুন ঘরে বসেই" stay as ad headlines; they change with each campaign, while the brand line stays. We never say that farmers can't get through to a hotline. We say they are trying and that distance makes it hard.
